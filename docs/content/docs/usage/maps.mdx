Barrelman publishes a ready-made map style for its basemap. Pass its URL to
[MapLibre GL JS](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/) and you have a map.

## Quickstart

```html
<link href="https://unpkg.com/maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl.css" rel="stylesheet" />
<script src="https://unpkg.com/maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl.js"></script>
<div id="map" style="height: 400px"></div>
<script>
  new maplibregl.Map({
    container: 'map',
    style: 'https://api.barrelman.dev/styles/basemap.json?api_key=YOUR_KEY',
    center: [-80.8431, 35.2271],
    zoom: 12,
  })
</script>
```

Use `basemap-dark.json` for a dark map.

A map library fetches the style and its tiles itself and cannot set an
`Authorization` header, so the key goes in the URL. It is visible to anyone who
opens the page. Give a browser key the `tiles` scope and nothing else, and
restrict it to your site's origins (see [Accounts](/usage/accounts)).

The style and TileJSON documents cost no credits. Tiles are charged as usual;
see [Pricing](/usage/pricing).

## Attribution

The style's source points at `/tiles/basemap.json`, a
[TileJSON](https://github.com/mapbox/tilejson-spec/tree/master/3.0.0) document
that carries the credits the basemap requires. MapLibre reads them from there
and shows them in its attribution control:

> © OpenStreetMap contributors © OpenMapTiles

Keep the control on. If you need it smaller, use
`attributionControl: { compact: true }`. [Attribution](/usage/attribution) lists
every credit and when it applies.

## How the key travels

When the style is fetched with `?api_key=`, the key is written into its source
URL, and from there into the tile URLs of the TileJSON. Nothing else needs
configuring.

A key sent as a Bearer header is never written into a style or TileJSON, so it
cannot leak through a cached or shared document. If your app authenticates by
header, add the key to tile requests yourself with
[`transformRequest`](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/RequestTransformFunction/).

## Other sources

Every tile source and bundle has a TileJSON at `/tiles/<source>.json`:
`/tiles/detail.json`, `/tiles/indoor.json`, `/tiles/landmarks.json`, or a
composite such as `/tiles/buildings_3d,parking_areas.json`. Each one lists the
source's zoom range, bounds and `vector_layers`, and the credits it owes. Add
one to a style as a `vector` source:

```js
map.on('load', () => {
  map.addSource('detail', {
    type: 'vector',
    url: 'https://api.barrelman.dev/tiles/detail.json?api_key=YOUR_KEY',
  })
})
```

`/tiles/detail.json` adds the Mapterhorn terrain credit, because some of its
layers carry heights read from Mapterhorn.

## Glyphs and sprites

Both styles load their label fonts and icons from Barrelman:

- `glyphs` is `/fonts/{fontstack}/{range}.pbf`. The fonts are Noto Sans
  Regular, Bold and Italic from the [OpenMapTiles fonts](https://github.com/openmaptiles/fonts)
  build, under the [SIL Open Font License 1.1](https://openfontlicense.org).
- `sprite` is `/sprites/osm-bright` or `/sprites/dark-matter`, the icon sheets
  from each style's repository, at 1x and `@2x`.

These URLs take no key and cost nothing, since MapLibre requests them without
one. They are limited per address. You can point a style of your own at them.

Glyphs cover the ranges 0-255 through 8192-8447, which is U+0000 to U+20FF:
Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Armenian, Hebrew, Arabic, the Indic scripts, Thai,
Georgian and general punctuation, among others. Any other range, such as
Chinese, Japanese or Korean, answers an empty glyph set. MapLibre accepts it,
so labels in those scripts are left out and the rest of the map draws as
usual. A font stack is served by its first font that Barrelman carries, and a
stack that names none of them gets Noto Sans Regular.
